Clinical Induction: Understanding the Decision Tools

This portal integrates two internationally validated acute stroke prognostic algorithms and seven objective bedside performance metrics into a pragmatic workflow:

1. PREP2 (Upper Limb Recovery at 12 Weeks)

• Timing Window: Assessed at Day 3 (48–72 hours post-stroke).
• Primary Milestone: Predicts functional arm capacity at 12 Weeks (3 Months) post-stroke (Action Research Arm Test: ARAT 0–57).
• The SAFE Score: Sum of MRC strength for Shoulder Abduction (0–5) and Finger Extension (0–5). Total: 0–10.
• Role of Age: For patients with SAFE ≥ 5, Age <80 automatically predicts Excellent recovery. For Age ≥ 80, SAFE must be ≥8 for Excellent; otherwise Good.
• Evidence on the Non-TMS Fallback: The full research protocol incorporates Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) to assess Motor Evoked Potentials (MEPs) when SAFE < 5. Because acute bedside TMS is unavailable in routine ward practice, this tool implements the conservative clinical fallback:

Key Literature Context:
1. SAFE ≥ 5 (~60% of patients): Accuracy is 78%–80% using Age + SAFE alone; TMS is never required.
2. SAFE < 5 (Without TMS): Patients branch directly to the NIHSS score (<7 vs. ≥7). This accurately identifies the Poor category (≥7), but treating all patients with NIHSS < 7 as Limited represents a conservative floor. Stinear et al. (2017) demonstrated in non-TMS CART analyses that an NIHSS < 9 can yield Good or Limited outcomes because ~10%–15% of patients retain latent descending pathway integrity that cannot be identified without neurophysiology.

2. Refined TWIST Index (Mobility & Walking Timeline)

• Timing Window: Assessed at Day 7 (1 week post-stroke).
• Scoring (0–4 Points): Points are awarded for Age <80 (+1 pt), Knee Extension ≥ MRC Grade 3 (+1 pt), and Berg Balance Scale (≥16: +2 pts; 6–15: +1 pt; <6: +0 pts).
• Target Milestone (FAC ≥ 4): Predicts weeks required to achieve independent walking on flat ground without human physical assistance.

3. Functional Performance Measures & Cut-Offs

• TCT (0–100): Early bed mobility and trunk control screen (<50 indicates severe trunk impairment; ≥50 predicts higher likelihood of regaining walking).
• Grip Strength: Evaluates distal recovery against sound limb symmetry and sarcopenia cut-offs (<16 kg females / <27 kg males).
• 5xSTS & 30s-CST: Lower-limb functional power and endurance (>12s on 5xSTS or <8 reps on 30s-CST flags falls risk).
• TUG: Dynamic transfer, gait, and turning mobility (≤13.5s low community falls risk; >13.5s elevated falls risk; >30s dependent).
• 10MWT: Comfortable gait speed (10 m ÷ time in seconds): <0.40 m/s household, 0.40–0.80 m/s limited community, >0.80 m/s community ambulator.
• 6MWT: Walking endurance (<200 m household/limited, 200–299 m limited community, ≥300 m functional community endurance).
